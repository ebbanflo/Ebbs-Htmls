About Microcosm

v4 · Sim mode

Microcosm is a real-time agent-based simulation of microbial evolution. Thirty founder lineages of cells live in a circular petri dish, each carrying heritable traits — size, motility, sensor range, hunting drive, toxin resistance, dietary preference, and temperature optimum. Over generations, Gaussian mutation and natural selection drift these traits toward whichever strategies happen to survive the dish's pressures.

The cellular machinery models real biology. Movement obeys Stokes drag (cost scales as size × velocity²); maintenance obeys the square-cube law; nutrient uptake is diffusion-limited at the surface; predator cells engulf smaller cells via phagocytosis; metabolic waste accumulates in a local toxin field that decays over time. Resistant cells digest poorly. Sibling-lineage cells are recognized as kin and rarely hunted.

When energy collapses, cells sporulate — encyst into dormant spores with near-zero metabolism and predator immunity, awaiting nutrient signals to germinate. A separate stochastic process flips a small fraction of cells into the persister phenotype: temporarily quiescent, antibiotic-shielded, then reverting. Genes spread laterally through horizontal gene transfer, so trait values flow across lineage boundaries even though body ancestry stays clonal.

Bacteriophages drift through the dish hunting specific lineages. Cells acquire CRISPR immunity against witnessed lyses; that immunity then propagates via HGT, producing Red-Queen oscillations as each lineage rises, attracts its phage, crashes, immunizes, and recovers. Environmental events — famine, bloom, antibiotic — periodically disturb the equilibrium.

There is no goal. The dish is a window onto evolution doing its work: a non-trivial number of mutually-interacting selection pressures producing visible drift in real time. Hover a cell while paused to inspect its lineage, traits, infection state, and CRISPR spacer array. Open Analytics for population history, trait distributions, and lineage rosters.
